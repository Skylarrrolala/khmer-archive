# Fact-check: Num Korm (នំគម) and Num Bot (នំបត់)

Accessed: 2026-11-21. Archive text checked: `supabase/seed.sql` (national-museum-redesign copy), rows `នំគម` and `នំបត់`.
Khmer quotes are copied from the page; the English in brackets is my translation.

**Headline:** Both desserts are real and documented. Num Bot is **not** an invented dessert. It has its own entry in the *Modern Cambodian-English Dictionary* (1997), which describes a **salted** mung-bean filling, not a sweet one. Several archive claims are unsupported or contradicted by sources: the stupa symbolism, the regions, the "rare / gone from urban markets" claim, and the sweet coconut-cream filling of num bot. The Num Bot photo does not reliably show num bot.

---

## 1. Num Korm — នំគម

### 1.1 Identity & naming

- **Khmer spelling:** នំគម. It is the headword in Headley 1977 and Headley 1997 [R1, R2]. Chuon Nath's dictionary defines it under គម [R3]. Khmer news uses the same spelling [R4, R6, R10].
- **Pronunciation:** IPA *num kɔɔm* [R1, R2].
- **Recommended transliteration:** **num kom**. It follows the dictionary pronunciation and is the most common English spelling (Cambodge Mag, Cambodia Restaurant Association, Wikipedia). The archive's "Num Korm" is also used in published English, for example by the Phnom Penh Post [R7], so it can stay as an alternative.
- **Alternative spellings:** num khom, nom kom, num korm, nom korm [R11].
- **Meaning of the name:** In Chuon Nath's dictionary, គម (*kom*) means "hunchbacked". The cake is defined as one "shaped like a hunched back" [R3]. This is the only explanation of the name found in a reference work.

### 1.2 Photo correspondence (`num-korm.jpg`)

**Matches.** The photo shows pyramid-shaped banana-leaf parcels next to unwrapped pyramids of translucent, pale-tan dough. Two are cut open to show a brown, stranded filling consistent with coconut cooked with palm sugar. This fits the dictionary's "pyramidal cake … filling of sweetened coconut" [R1, R2] and Thmey Thmey's "coconut filling stirred with palm sugar … wrapped in banana leaf" [R4]. No sesame is visible, though that does not mean there is none. The photo's provenance is unknown (see CLAUDE.md photo-provenance rule).

### 1.5 Proposed narrative — Num Korm

Num kom (នំគម, also written num korm) is a steamed cake of glutinous rice flour dough wrapped around a filling of grated coconut cooked with palm sugar. Some recipes add toasted sesame seeds. Each cake is enclosed in banana leaf and folded into a pyramid before steaming, and Cambodian–English dictionaries record it as a pyramidal cake, often filled with sweetened coconut.

The name is descriptive. In the Khmer dictionary of the Supreme Patriarch Chuon Nath, *kom* means hunched, and the cake is defined as one shaped like a hunched back. The same entry allows a sweetened bean filling in place of coconut.

Num kom belongs to everyday life as well as to ritual. It is among the leaf-wrapped cakes offered at pagodas for Pchum Ben and Khmer New Year, and it appears at weddings alongside num ansom, the cylindrical rice cake. A Cambodian historian and several Khmer writers read the pair as female and male. The interpretation is widely repeated, but it remains a reading rather than a documented origin.

The cake is still easy to find, sold in Phnom Penh's markets and by makers who supply hotels and restaurants.

### 1.6 Proposed occasion line — Num Korm

Pchum Ben and Khmer New Year offerings; weddings, beside num ansom; everyday market sweet.

## 2. Num Bot — នំបត់

### 2.1 Identity & naming

- **Num Bot is a real, documented cake.** It has its own headword in Headley 1997: "kind of cake made of sticky rice flour filled with salted mung beans and wrapped in banana leaves" [R2].
- **Khmer spelling:** នំបត់ [R2, R4, R5, R6, R10].
- **Pronunciation:** IPA *num bɑt* [R2].
- **Recommended transliteration:** **num bot**. Alternatives: **num bort** (Phnom Penh Post [R7]) and nom bat.
- **Meaning of "bot":** បត់ is a verb meaning "to fold, bend, crease, pleat" [R2]. Chuon Nath gives "បត់ស្លឹក" ("to fold a leaf") as a usage example [R3]. The word meaning is therefore confirmed. **No source says the cake is named for folding.** That is a plausible inference, not a documented etymology.
- **Overlap with num kom.** Sabay News heads its entry "នំបត់ ឬនំគម" ("num bot or num kom"), treating the two as one cake [R5]. Its text, however, is lifted almost word for word from Thmey Thmey 25 [R4], which lists num bot and num kom as **separate** cakes. Headley 1997 [R2] and Kampuchea Thmey [R10] also treat them as distinct. AMS [R6] uses them interchangeably in one ritual slot ("num kom or num bot"). Best reading: two distinct cakes that are closely related and sometimes conflated or substituted for each other.

### 2.2 Photo correspondence (`num-bot.jpg`)

**Not a reliable match. Hedged.**

- The basket holds **two different parcel shapes**. At the back are tall, pyramid-shaped leaf parcels. Dictionaries describe that shape for **num kom** ("pyramidal") [R1, R2]. At the front are plump, oblong, pillow-shaped packets with tucked ends.
- The one captioned num bot photo found is in Thmey Thmey 25 [R4], credited "រូបភាពដោយលោក វិចិត្រ" ("photo by Mr. Vichet"). It shows a basket of **flat**, irregular, roughly square-to-triangular folded banana-leaf parcels. That matches the article's "ខ្ចប់រាងសំប៉ែតៗ" ("wrapped in flat shapes"). The parcels are darkened from steaming, and none is pyramidal or pillow-shaped like those in the archive photo.
- The archive photo is styled (frangipani, a folded-leaf border), and its provenance is unknown. A photograph cannot show the filling.
- **Conclusion:** the photo looks like an assortment of wrapped cakes, and its pyramidal parcels resemble num kom. It should not be captioned as num bot without confirmation from whoever took it. Replace it with a family photograph if possible.

### 2.5 Proposed narrative — Num Bot

Num bot (នំបត់, also romanised num bort) is a steamed cake of glutinous rice flour enclosing a mung bean filling, wrapped in banana leaf. The Modern Cambodian–English Dictionary describes the filling as salted mung beans, and a Khmer-language survey of popular cakes describes the parcels as flat. In Khmer, *bot* means to fold or bend, and Chuon Nath's dictionary gives "to fold a leaf" as an example. No source consulted explains how the cake came by its name.

Num bot is closely related to num kom. The two are listed side by side among the cakes wrapped and offered to monks around Pchum Ben. At weddings, either num kom or num bot may be set beside num ansom on the offering tray used in the sampeah phtim rite. Some popular writing treats the two names as one cake, while dictionaries and most listings keep them distinct.

Sources do not describe num bot as rare. In 2021 it was reported as mostly sold in markets and served at ceremonies in Phnom Penh, and in 2023 a Phnom Penh maker listed it among his wrapped cakes. Its regional history, and whether it is usually eaten sweet or savoury, remain to be documented.

### 2.6 Proposed occasion line — Num Bot

Pchum Ben offerings to monks; weddings (beside num ansom, in place of or with num kom); market sweet in Phnom Penh.

### 2.7 Unresolved — Num Bot

- **Sweet or savoury?** Headley 1997 says "salted mung beans", but the archive text says a sweet coconut-and-sugar filling. To settle it: the family's own recipe, or a Khmer recipe source that names num bot and lists its ingredients.
- **Etymology of the name** (folding?). No source states it. To settle it: Chuon Nath's own entry for នំបត់ (SEAlang returned only Headley for that compound), or Royal Academy of Cambodia material.
- **Exact shape** (rectangular vs. square or triangular flat folds). To settle it: more captioned photographs, or what the family describes.
- **Regions** (Battambang, Prey Veng). No evidence found. To settle it: the family's account, or provincial sources.
- **Whether num bot and num kom are sometimes the same cake under two names.** Sabay equates them; most sources do not. To settle it: a Khmer ethnographic or Royal Academy source.
